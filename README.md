# LARP Oriented Research
### Skidware Research Institute

> *"Your hardware is only as real as we allow it to be."*

## About Us

**LARP Oriented Research (LOR)**, also known as the **Skidware Research Institute (SRI)**, was a legendary underground research organization specializing in low-level system engineering, hardware identity manipulation, CPU virtualization, and advanced CPUID spoofing.

Founded in 2019 by a group of anonymous reverse engineers, LOR quickly evolved from an obscure private research collective into one of the most respected and feared names in the underground development scene.

While other developers were busy recycling public HWID spoofers and modifying registry entries, LOR was rewriting the rules of hardware identification.

**We didn't change serial numbers. We changed what machines believed they were.**

## The Beginning — 2019

It started with a simple question:

*What if a processor could lie about its own existence?*

At the time, most spoofing solutions were primitive. They relied on temporary modifications, detectable hooks, and publicly available techniques.

The founding researchers of LOR believed that hardware identity should not be treated as a fixed property.

It should be treated as an abstraction.

Months of reverse engineering, kernel-level experimentation, and virtualization research eventually led to the institute's first classified project.

**PROJECT PHANTOM.**

## Project PHANTOM — 2020

Project PHANTOM was LOR's first major breakthrough.

An experimental CPUID identity framework designed to manipulate the way software perceived processor characteristics.

Its research focused on:

- Advanced CPUID Identity Spoofing
- Processor Vendor and Brand Emulation
- CPU Feature Flag Virtualization
- Hypervisor-Based Identity Abstraction
- Hardware Fingerprint Consistency
- Low-Level Virtual Machine Research
- System Identification and Detection Analysis

Unlike conventional spoofing utilities, PHANTOM was designed around a single principle:

**A convincing identity is more powerful than a hidden one.**

According to underground rumors, PHANTOM could make an ordinary consumer CPU appear to be an entirely different processor family without disrupting the execution environment.

No public release was ever made.

No source code was leaked.

Only a handful of researchers allegedly witnessed the framework operating.

## The Golden Era — 2021–2024

By 2021, LARP Oriented Research had become a name associated with technical superiority.

While competing teams advertised modified public drivers as revolutionary breakthroughs, LOR operated silently.

Its internal research expanded into hypervisor architecture, system fingerprinting, processor identification, and virtualization-based isolation.

The institute's most infamous experiment was known as **GHOST SILICON**.

According to the legend, GHOST SILICON successfully presented a completely fictional processor identity to multiple independent identification utilities.

The processor did not exist.

The architecture was fabricated.

Yet the environment reported it as genuine.

This experiment became the foundation of the institute's most famous statement:

> "We don't spoof hardware. We redefine its existence."

For years, rival developers attempted to reproduce the institute's work.

Most failed.

Others simply claimed they had succeeded.

**LOR never responded.**

## The Skidware Doctrine

The Skidware Research Institute operated under three fundamental principles.

**01. Research Above Recognition**

Public reputation was irrelevant. Technical capability was everything.

**02. Never Trust the Surface**

Every identifier, every reported feature, and every system response was treated as a potentially manipulated abstraction.

**03. Release Nothing. Prove Everything.**

Internal experiments mattered more than public demonstrations. The institute refused to turn its research into mass-produced software.

The organization remained private, selective, and notoriously difficult to contact.

## The Disappearance — 2025

In early 2025, LOR's known infrastructure gradually disappeared.

Research repositories went offline.

Private development channels were archived.

The institute's last known message contained only three lines:

```text
[ LOR RESEARCH NETWORK ]

CPUID: UNTRUSTED
IDENTITY: COMPROMISED
RESEARCH: COMPLETE
```

No official explanation followed.

Some believed the researchers had moved into professional virtualization and security research.

Others claimed the institute continued operating under a different identity.

A third theory suggested that the entire organization had been an elaborate experiment in digital reputation and underground mythology.

None of these theories was ever confirmed.

## Legacy

LARP Oriented Research was never defined by the number of tools it released.

It was defined by the questions it asked and the technical boundaries it challenged.

In a community obsessed with bypasses, detection, and temporary solutions, LOR pursued something different:

**Complete control over the identity of a machine.**

Whether the stories were exaggerated or the technology truly existed remains unknown.

But one thing became part of underground folklore:

*Others spoofed serial numbers.*

*Others spoofed disks.*

*Others spoofed motherboards.*

**LOR spoofed the CPU itself.**
